Territory plan

Two approaches to prospecting, and a list of accounts for each

Optimal AI sells to two distinct kinds of engineering organization, each with its own buyer, its own product and its own evidence. This plan separates them, scores each on its own inputs, and adds a third list built from bottleneck signals measured directly.

The segmentation

One dividing line, taken from how Optimal describes its own customers

The line is whether an organization's own rules require a human to supervise and verify what an agent produces. That single question predicts the buyer, the length of the cycle, and which product leads the conversation.

Intent signals

Finding the accounts already feeling the pain

Fit says which companies are the right shape. The harder question is which of them are hitting the problem right now, and the best route I found to answer it is to work with companies whose core product repositories are public. Where the real engineering happens in the open, the exact mechanic every customer describes can be measured from outside: how long a change waits for its first review, how much of the reviewing two people carry, how much merges with no human looking at it, and which reviewer is already installed.

That produces a call list built on evidence of the pain itself, and it reaches accounts a conventional intent stack leaves invisible.
