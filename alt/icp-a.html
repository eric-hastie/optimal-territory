ICP A: velocity accounts

Twenty-five teams shipping far above their headcount, where the review queue is the one thing that did not scale. Sourced as structural twins of existing customers and ranked on team shape, engineering density and shipping cadence.

Reading this list. The fit score ranks team shape, engineering density, data sensitivity and distribution. The why column is the opening line: how big the engineering team is against the company, whether review crosses timezones, and what obligations the code carries. Hover any column heading for what it is, how it was collected, and how to read it.
v0

Voice companies carry a regulatory boost. EU AI Act transparency obligations took effect on 2 August 2026 and apply directly to voice and speech products, which is a dated, external reason to talk now. The scoring model is an early version, still moving: the weights are a starting point and the inputs are being filled in as collection continues. It is published so the reasoning can be checked.

A value shown with a leading < is an upper bound derived from engineering being absent from the top-5 functions, not a measured figure.Read it as: Engineering headcount comes from the people facet alone. Where it is absent, the cell stays empty until the figure is collected.Eng share?Eng shareEngineering as a percentage of total staff.How: Engineering headcount divided by total staff, both from the LinkedIn people facet.Read it as: Fifteen engineers in a 236-person company is a different organisation from fifteen in a thirty-person company. This column is what tells those apart.Metro?MetroMetro bucket used to segment the board into dinner-event guest lists.How: Derived from HQ, then banded by metro_confidence (proxy-ok, moderate, weak).Read it as: Inferred from HQ, not from headcount by city. A company HQ'd in Austin with a twenty-person SF engineering office currently scores zero. Metro is scored as a band.ICP fit?ICP fitHow well the account matches the kind of company Optimal AI sells to. Structural, changes quarterly, and kept separate from the Intent score, which answers the timing question.How: Six weighted terms scored 0-100 by tools/icp_score.py: engineering headcount band (42), SCM present (18), engineering share of staff (15), data sensitivity (10), distributed engineering (8), staff by metro (7). Weights sum to 100, so the score reads as a percentage of achievable fit.Read it as: A term with no data scores ZERO by decision, so read a low score next to the ceiling: the gap between them is the part still to be collected. Weights are a starting point: halving or doubling any one of them moves the average account only one to six places.Evidence?EvidenceThe reason each term scored what it scored, term by term.
