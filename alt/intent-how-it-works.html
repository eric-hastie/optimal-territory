Intent signals

What review load looks like from outside

Fit says which accounts are the right shape. This says which ones are hitting the bottleneck right now, measured in their own code activity.

01

The bottleneck is countable

Where a company's public repositories are its product, the exact mechanic your customers describe can be counted: how long a change waits for its first review, how much of the reviewing two people carry, how much merges unreviewed, and how much of the backlog has sat open a month or more.

02

Volume against capacity is the direction that matters

A step change in pull request volume, a rising share of agent-authored branches and new contributors arriving all point the same way: load climbing against a review capacity that is fixed.

03

Context decides what a number means

The share of merges with no human review is the sharpest measure available, and it reads in opposite directions depending on whether a review tool is already running, so it is always read next to the reviewer-installed column.

04

A reviewer already installed is the warmest state

Two of the seven published customers arrived by replacing a tool that was already there, one of them over false positives. Where a competing reviewer is running, budget exists, the category is understood, and the conversation opens at comparison.

05

Applied only where the code is visible

Signals are computed only where a company's engineers work in public, measured as the share of the engineering team that appears in the public repositories, so every row on the list rests on activity we can see.
