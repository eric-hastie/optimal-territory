Territory plan

Two approaches to prospecting, and a list of accounts for each

Optimal AI sells to two distinct kinds of engineering organization. This plan separates them, scores each on its own inputs, and adds a third list built from bottleneck signals measured directly.

The segmentation

Two ICP lanes, taken from how Optimal describes its own customers

ICP A

Velocity

Wedge: output has outrun review

Leads withOptibot

Small teams shipping far above their headcount, where agents write an increasingly large share of code and the review queue is not scaling to match. Adoption can start with a single engineer on a single repository.

Buying runs bottom-up: an engineer adopts it, colleagues follow, and leadership joins once the value is already visible. The strongest published references come from here, and five of the seven case studies are Optibot stories about review throughput.

ICP B

Governance

Wedge: evidence, security and visibility

Leads withInsights, then Optibot

Regulated organizations where every agent output is supervised and verified before it ships. Here the review layer is how the obligation gets met, and the record of what was reviewed is worth as much as the review.

Both large published customers bought Insights. One replaced spreadsheet reporting with a single trusted view across Jira and GitHub; the other turned real engineering activity into audit-ready billing and cost capitalization. InfoSec opens the conversation, and the buying unit is one named team inside a much larger organization.

"we have some really large customers... not allowed to use multi-stream agents or even have work in the background. It always has to be supervised and needs to be on screen and everything that's being outputted needs to be verified... they're in regulated spaces" Git Sh*t Done LIVE, Ep 3

The lists

Three lists, three different approaches to prospecting

Two lists rank fit, each on the model built for its own segment. The third ranks measured signal, and finds accounts by measuring the bottleneck directly in public code activity.

List 1

ICP A: velocity

25 accounts

Every account is confirmed to be building or shipping with a named coding agent, evidenced in its own requisitions or on its own site. Where an account is also a structural twin of a published customer, that customer is named beside it with the match.

Ranked on team shape, engineering density and shipping cadence, so what decides it is how much a team ships against how many people are available to review it.

List 2

ICP B: governance

25 accounts

Built from public registries that carry a dated, checkable obligation: compliance certifications, healthcare and lending regulatory footprints, insurance and government platforms, and payment processors operating in PCI scope.

Drawn across seven sourcing routes so the list spans the segment, and sized against Optimal's own enterprise references. Optimal's enterprise customers bought for a single team inside a large organization, so the play is to prospect into these accounts and land one team at a time. Ranked on compliance posture, procurement surface and scale.

List 3

Public intent signals

25 accounts

Companies whose own public repositories are their product, which makes the review bottleneck countable from outside: how long a change waits for its first review, how much of the reviewing two people carry, how much merges unreviewed, and which reviewer is already installed.

Every one of these comes from review behavior itself, which means we can find companies already feeling measurable pain from the outside, before anyone has told us about it. That is the list to call this week.

Status

What is built, and what comes next

Two scoring models and three account lists are built and live on this site. The models are a starting point, intended to be refined and improved with data from enterprise data tools and with manual adjustments to the inputs and their weights.

Next is depth per account: the named team inside each governance organization, security hiring as a live signal, and a weekly refresh of the intent list. Once the ICP model is production-ready, my next step is building the messaging sequences and cadences for each target persona.
