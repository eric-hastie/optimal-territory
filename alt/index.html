Territory plan

Two ways into this market, and the accounts in each

Optimal AI sells into two situations, each with its own buyer, its own product and its own evidence. This plan separates them, scores each on its own terms, and adds a third list built from bottleneck signals that can be measured directly.

The segmentation

One dividing line, taken from how Optimal describes its own customers

The line is whether an organisation's own rules require a human to supervise and verify what an agent produces. That single question predicts the buyer, the length of the cycle, and which product leads the conversation.

ICP A

Velocity

Wedge: output has outrun review

Leads withOptibot

Small teams shipping far above their headcount, where agents write most of the non-critical code and the review queue is the one thing that did not scale. Adoption can start with a single engineer on a single repository.

Buying runs bottom-up: an engineer adopts it, colleagues follow, and leadership joins once the value is already visible. Short cycles, and the strongest published references come from here. Five of the seven case studies are Optibot stories about review throughput.

Governance

Wedge: evidence, security and visibility

Leads withInsights, then Optibot

Regulated organisations where every agent output is supervised and verified before it ships. Here the review layer is how the obligation gets met, and the record of what was reviewed is worth as much as the review.

Both large published customers bought Insights. InfoSec opens the conversation, and the buying unit is one named team inside a much larger organisation.

The lists

Three lists, three different approaches to prospecting

Two lists rank fit, each on the model built for its own segment. The third ranks evidence, and finds accounts by measuring the bottleneck directly in public code activity. Each list answers a different sourcing question, so each keeps its own method.

List 1

ICP A: velocity

25 accounts

Sourced as structural twins of existing customers: the same buyer, the same class of data, the same shape of team. Every row carries the customer it resembles and the evidence for the match.

Ranked on team shape, engineering density and shipping cadence, so the deciding factor is how much a team ships against how many people are available to review it.

List 3

Public intent signals

25 accounts

Companies whose own repositories are their product, which makes the bottleneck countable from outside: review latency, how much of the reviewing two people carry, merges that shipped with no human review, and which reviewer is already installed.

This is intent measured in a company's own activity, and it is the kind of signal a standard enrichment stack does not carry. Clay reaches contributors and contacts; review behaviour has to be computed. It answers who to call this week.
