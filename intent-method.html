Intent signals, in detail

How intent signals are measured

Every measurement pulled from public repositories, how the four measures are built from them, and the gate that decides which accounts they may be applied to at all.

What is measured

Every row is a measurement pulled from public repositories, and the customer statement it maps to.

Four measures, and one total for ordering

Each is scored 0 to 100 against ranges observed across the measured population.

MeasureWeight in the totalWhat it answers
Bottleneck45How constrained review is right now: latency, concentration, unreviewed merges, self-merges, large changes, stale backlog.
Pressure25Whether load is outgrowing capacity: volume step, agent-authored branches, new contributors, timezone spread.
Escape20Whether bad code already reaches production: reverts and hotfixes.
Readiness10Timing only, from what is already installed.

The components stay visible next to the total, because they carry different plays. A high bottleneck with low pressure is a team that is already stuck. The reverse is a team about to be.

The validity gate

The single most important control on this list, and the reason it is short.

Pull requests per engineer, measured from public repositories, describes how much a company builds in public. Ranking on it produces a leaderboard of open-source tooling companies and nothing else. The discriminator that works is the share of a company's engineers who appear in its public repositories at all.

BandMeaningTreatment
MaterialMost engineers touch public repositories. The repository is the product.Signals apply.
PartialMixed.Signals apply with caution.
InvisibleSDKs, examples and docs. Not the codebase.Signals are nulled, never scored low.

What the material segment actually measures. Its pull request flow includes outside contributors, so for open-source projects this measures maintainer review burden. That is a real bottleneck, arguably the clearest form of it, and each row is labeled with which of the two it describes.

What is already installed

Three states only, and a fourth that was removed.

A tool counts as deployed only when it is running at real volume, across a meaningful share of pull requests, and recently. Anything below that bar is recorded as traces and left unscored, since a handful of reviews spread over a year reads the same as idle curiosity. None is exactly that.

On a reviewer that goes quiet. It looks like the warmest possible moment, and it reads the same from outside as a team that bought the tool and moved the work into private repositories. One account showed fourteen reviews across more than eight hundred pull requests over fourteen months, which fits both stories equally well, so the state is recorded and left out of the score.

The control row

An existing customer with public repositories is carried through every scoring run. Whatever the model says, it has to describe that account as one that already solved its review problem. It has caught two scoring errors that otherwise looked like perfectly plausible numbers.
