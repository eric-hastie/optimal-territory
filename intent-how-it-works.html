Intent signals

How I think about intent

Fit says who is worth working. Intent says who is worth calling this week. It is measured rather than inferred, and these five rules are what keep it honest.

01

Measure the bottleneck, do not infer it from hiring

Most prospecting models treat open engineering roles as buying intent: more hiring, more need. That logic inverts here. Your own thesis is that a static team now produces several times the code it used to, so the account with no open roles and a tripled pull request volume is the textbook fit, and the hiring-led model scores it near zero.

02

Only where the repository is the product

A public GitHub organisation is not the same as public work. Plenty of companies publish SDKs, docs and examples while every line that matters stays private. Signals are only computed where public activity is genuinely the company's engineering, and everywhere else they are recorded as unmeasurable rather than as low.

03

A metric is not a signal until it is read in context

The share of merges that received no human review is the clearest bottleneck measure there is, and on its own it is ambiguous: a team with no review capacity and a team that already installed a review bot look identical. The same number means opposite things depending on what else is running.

04

A reviewer already installed is good news

The instinct is to treat a competing tool as a closed door. The evidence says the opposite: budget exists, the category is understood, and two of your seven published customers arrived by replacing something that was already there. The conversation starts at comparison instead of explanation.

05

If two explanations fit the data, it is not a signal

I built a churn signal, meaning a review bot that went quiet, and then removed it. From outside, a team that abandoned a tool and a team that moved the work into private repositories look exactly the same. A measurement that cannot distinguish those two is not evidence, however good the story sounds.

What this buys

A weekly call list built on what companies are actually doing, not on what a database says about them. These are the signals a standard enrichment stack does not carry, because they have to be computed from activity rather than looked up.
