Fit scoring

How I determined ICP for each account

I took what I learned from Optimal's website, all seven case studies, and five podcast episodes, then worked with Claude to pull the patterns into an ICP profile.

I found that the buyers form two groups: small AI-native teams adopting Optibot bottom-up because review cannot keep up with what they are shipping, and large regulated organizations buying Insights because someone has to sign off on what went out the door. Scoring them with one model buries the smaller group (I tried it, and the whole top of the list came back regulated), so I split them into "ICP A" and "ICP B" and built a model for each.

This page walks through the inputs that matter for each one, and why. The link at the bottom shows the calculations (it is a v0 - the weights are a starting point, not a finished answer, and I would want to revise them against real win data in my first 90 days).

The ICP A score

Five weighted inputs summing to 100, so a score reads as a percentage of achievable fit.

Engineering headcount band 50, distributed engineering 22, data sensitivity 16, staff in the event metros 6, supported platform confirmed 6. Two boosts sit on top and are recorded separately: +10 where an account is a competitor or structural twin of a named customer, since the reference already exists, and +10 where a live regulatory clock applies, currently the EU AI Act transparency duties on voice products.

The ICP B score

Five weighted inputs summing to 100, so a score reads as a percentage of achievable fit.

Compliance posture 40, agent adoption 20, procurement surface 20, scale 15, sourcing route strength 5. Compliance carries the most because it is the one attribute every account in this segment shares, and the one the buyer is obliged to act on. No boosts sit on top here: a customer lookalike is already carried by the sourcing route, and a regulatory clock applies to every account on the list, so neither would separate one row from another.
